The problem

Sixteen junctions, sixteen minds

We simulate a city of 16 traffic lights on a 4×4 grid in SUMO, a standard traffic simulator. Each junction is controlled by its own AI agent, trained with reinforcement learning.

Every agent sees only its own junction: the queue on each of its four roads, which direction is currently green, and how long it has been green. From that, it chooses which direction to turn green next.

The question: do agents that can send messages to their neighbours control traffic better, and can they invent those messages themselves instead of using a format we designed?

Each dot is a junction with its own agent. The highlighted one sees the four queues feeding into it.

Robustness · demand surge

Under a 3× surge, simple wins

A sudden tripling of demand the agents never saw in training. We report this honestly: here the fixed-time controller is best, because long fixed greens give more capacity.

E1 (left) against fixed-time (right) during the surge.

Robustness · sensor failure

When a junction goes blind

All four sensors at junction B1 break and read zero cars. Real signals handle this by falling back to a fixed timer for that junction. We tested both: leaving the AI running, and switching on the fallback.

E1 with junction B1 blind (left) against the same with the fixed-time fallback (right). The broken junction is marked in red in the viewer.

Priority · emergency vehicles

Clearing the way for an ambulance

A city controller is not only about average waiting time. We mark one vehicle as an ambulance, and when it approaches a junction the agent can give its direction priority, so the lights turn green ahead of it and its lane clears before it arrives.

The lights on the ambulance's path switch to green just before it reaches them, instead of making it stop and wait in the queue. This is a safety behaviour on top of the normal traffic optimisation, not a replacement for it.

The agent keeps running the whole network at the same time, so giving one vehicle priority should cost the other directions as little as possible. The full comparison, with exact numbers for the ambulance's travel time and the effect on everyone else, is being finalised with the latest results and will be added here.

Generalisation

Trained small, runs big

We trained E1 on a small 2×2 grid, then dropped it onto the 4×4 city with no retraining at all. It still beats the fixed-time controllers.
